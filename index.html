03 / WHAT EACH PIECE DOES

Know the handoffs,
not just the names.

The key idea is what information or decision passes from one stage to the next.

01 / PLAN

Position before person

HR and a manager define the work, reporting line, location, and budget. When Position Management is used in Employee Central (EC), an approved position can become the basis for a recruiting requisition.

Handoff

Approved position → job requisition

02 / ATTRACT & SELECT

From applicant to offer

Recruiting Marketing can help present jobs and build interest. Recruiting Management handles requisitions, applications, interview steps, and offers. Marketing is optional in this conceptual route.

Handoff

Accepted offer → onboarding initiation

03 / WELCOME & HIRE

Prepare, then make it official

Onboarding coordinates pre-start information and tasks for the new hire, manager, and HR. Candidate and onboarding data can flow into EC, where HR validates the hire and maintains the employment record.

Handoff

New-hire details → employee record

04 / OPERATE

Keep the record dependable

EC stores core people, job, and organization data. Its Time Off capabilities manage absences. SuccessFactors Time Tracking adds attendance and working-time needs; Employee Central Payroll processes pay where adopted.

Remember

Time, absence, and payroll are related, distinct processes.

05 / GROW & REWARD

A repeating conversation

Performance & Goals supports goals, feedback, and reviews. Learning supports training. Career and Talent Development / Succession supports future roles. Compensation supports reward planning; it does not run payroll.

Handoff

Goals and reviews → development and reward decisions

06 / TRANSITION & EXIT

Change role or leave well

Transfers and promotions update the job record in EC and may trigger new goals or learning. For a departure, EC records termination; the Offboarding process in SuccessFactors Onboarding can coordinate handover, equipment, and exit tasks.

Handoff

Termination event → offboarding activities

◎
ACROSS ALL STAGES

Analytics asks what the pattern means.

Workforce analytics and planning draw on HR data to explore questions such as headcount, turnover, hiring time, skills, and future capacity. They inform decisions throughout the journey rather than appearing only after learning or before payroll.

04 / PUT IT TOGETHER

Follow one employee.

A simple example makes the module boundaries easier to remember.

A

Meet Asha

Asha joins a growing team, develops into a new role, and later leaves the company.

Illustrative scenario
  1. 01
    A role opens.

    The manager and HR define it in EC Position Management, if the company uses positions.

  2. 02
    Asha applies and accepts.

    Recruiting handles her application and offer. Onboarding starts her paperwork and welcome tasks.

  3. 03
    Her employment begins.

    EC holds the approved hire and job details. Time, absence, and payroll processes use relevant data.

  4. 04
    She grows.

    Goals and reviews inform learning, career discussion, and possible compensation planning. A promotion updates EC.

  5. 05
    She departs.

    EC records the termination. Offboarding coordinates handover and exit tasks; reporting can examine turnover trends.

05 / A USEFUL DISTINCTION

Journey order ≠
implementation order.

One of the reference images shows an implementation sequence. That is a project-planning question, while this page follows an employee’s experience.

EMPLOYEE JOURNEY

What happens to a person?

Plan → recruit → onboard → employ → develop → transition or exit.

IMPLEMENTATION SEQUENCE

What might a project set up first?

SAP recommends starting with Employee Central when integrating it with Recruiting and Onboarding, so foundational people and organizational data is in place.
